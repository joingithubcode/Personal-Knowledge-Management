# deep-work-principles

## Purpose

Explain deep work and the conditions that make focused cognitive effort
possible.

## Context

Hard creative and technical work needs long, undisturbed concentration, but
interruptions and shallow tasks fragment attention. Deep work is the practice
of guarding time for such focus. It produces the highest-value output, and it
depends on deliberate environment and habit design.

## Main Notes

- Deep work is focused, undistracted effort on cognitively demanding tasks.
- Shallow work includes email, meetings, and quick replies; it is easy but
  low value.
- The payoff of deep work grows with practice; attention is a trainable
  skill.
- Schedule deep work like an appointment and protect the time from
  interruption.
- Work in long blocks with clear goals; short, scattered sessions do not
  reach the depth.
- Remove distractions: silence notifications, close tabs, and make the
  shallow work wait.
- Rituals and routines lower the cost of starting: a fixed place and time
  help.
- Rest and recovery matter; focus degrades without breaks and sleep.

## References

- Foundational concept; no single source.
- Popularized by Cal Newport in "Deep Work", 2016.
