# statistics-for-data-science

## Purpose

Explain the statistical ideas that data science relies on to draw
conclusions from data.

## Context

Data science works from samples, not whole populations, and samples can
mislead. Statistics supplies the tools for describing data and deciding
whether patterns are real or chance. Without it, charts and models hide more
than they reveal.

## Main Notes

- Descriptive statistics summarize a sample: mean, median, and mode for
  center; range, variance, and standard deviation for spread.
- The distribution of a variable shows how values are spread and where they
  cluster.
- Inferential statistics draws conclusions about a population from a
  sample.
- Hypothesis testing asks whether an observed effect could be chance,
  producing a p-value for that assessment.
- A p-value is not the probability that the hypothesis is true; it is the
  probability of the data under the hypothesis, and it is widely misread.
- Confidence intervals give a range likely to contain the true value, with a
  stated level of confidence.
- Correlation measures how two variables move together, but correlation is
  not causation.
- Data science uses statistics alongside machine learning to validate that
  models generalize and effects are real.
