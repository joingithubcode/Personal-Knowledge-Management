# probability-basics

## Purpose

Explain the foundations of probability needed for data work.

## Context

Data is full of uncertainty: future values, random samples, and noisy
measurements. Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty, giving rules
for describing how likely events are and how they combine. Machine learning
and statistics both build on it.

## Main Notes

- Probability assigns a number from 0 to 1 to how likely an event is.
- The probability of all possible outcomes sums to 1.
- A random variable takes values from a distribution rather than a fixed
  value.
- Independence means one event gives no information about another; dependent
  events require joint probability.
- Conditional probability measures an event given that another happened;
  Bayes' rule flips the conditioning.
- Common distributions include the normal (bell curve), uniform, and
  binomial; each models a class of phenomena.
- Expectation is the long-run average of a random variable; variance
  measures how spread out it is.
- The law of large numbers explains why averages settle down with more
  samples.
